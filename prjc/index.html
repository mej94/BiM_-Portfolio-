A five storey civic office building with a curved glazed front, an entrance plinth and a car park, first drawn as a learning model for a site in Uganda and documented here against a notional UK client. The model was rich in geometry and poor in information: 56 warnings, 521 unused elements, four floor plan DWGs embedded as imports, placeholder project information and no sheets in use. I made it fit to share under ISO 19650, then wrote down the rules that keep it that way.

Model audit

Audit and cleanup of an inherited model

609
unused elements purged in three passes: 494, then 60, then 55

01What was wrong

The model held 327 walls and 139 doors but no rooms, two empty sheets with no title block, four floor plan DWGs imported instead of linked, and project information that still read Project Number and Enter address here. A team receiving it would spend its first day cleaning.

02What I decided

Work on a copy named to the container convention and leave the original untouched. Delete the imported DWGs instead of converting them to links: imports carry their own layers and line styles into the model and can never be updated. Purge until nothing is left, because removing a family frees its materials and patterns for the next pass. Keep the title block type and all 13 view templates for the sheet work.

03What changed

Every change ran as a named Revit transaction, so each step sits separately in the Undo history.

04What I would do next

Be straight about file size. The original read 29.7 MB, but the working copy opened at 24.2 MB because Save As compacts the file. The purge took it to 23.1 MB. Next time I record size after a compact save, so the cleanup only gets credit for what it did.

Warning resolution

Warnings reduced from 56 to 7 with documented exceptions

56→7
warnings, cut by 87 percent without moving a wall

01What was wrong

56 warnings. 32 came from quad corner mullions at curtain wall ends that join nothing. 11 walls were attached to the pent slab while their tops were already set by level and offset, so the attachment pointed at a slab they never touched. Three stepped slabs overlapped the ground slab.

02What I decided

Fix the relationship before the geometry. Detach the walls, which moves nothing. Make a rectangular mullion with the same 150 by 150 section and swap the 32 corners to it. The stepped slabs looked like errors, so I confirmed the design intent before touching them: they are entrance plinths at 0, minus 150 and minus 270, so they stay and get joined, plinth cutting. For each duplicate wall I checked what it hosted and kept the one carrying the door.

03What changed

56 to 13 after the walls and mullions, 9 after the plinth joins, 7 after the duplicates. The joins also stop a quantity takeoff counting the overlapping concrete twice.

04What I would do next

Rebuild the 15 in place casework families as loadable families so they can be scheduled and reused. They are the last thing in this model a second team would curse.

Why keep seven

Zero was reachable by deleting things that mattered. Straightening an off axis wall drags every joined wall with it, and changing the ramp changes its gradient, which is a design decision. A justified residue is what a BIM manager wants to read. A clean zero usually hides a deletion.

Interview story

The plinth slabs. Asking before changing geometry, and knowing the overlap would have overstated the concrete.

Information standards

Project information, BEP extract and document register

3→0
placeholder fields in Project Information, replaced by ISO 19650 codes

01What was wrong

Project information held placeholders: Project Number, Enter address here, Project Status. Nothing in the file said who made it, what state it was in, or what it was called, so every title block and IFC export would have carried blanks.

02What I decided

ISO 19650 sets the codes, not the Revit fields, so I put each code where Revit can pass it on: originator MEJ in Organization, City Council Offices as the building name for IfcBuilding, S0 as the status, PRJC as the project code. Then a BEP extract covering naming, status and revision codes, CDE gates, level of information need and the checks a model passes before it is shared.

03What changed

A register of four containers. The model sits at P01.02 because P01.01 was the state I received; it becomes P01 when shared.

04What I would do next

Two loose ends before the register goes out. The project number still read PRJC 001 at the audit, and codes carry no spaces. Sheet A101 Unnamed holds no views and needs a purpose or deleting.
