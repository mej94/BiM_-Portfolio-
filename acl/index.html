A three storey detached house in the UK for client Barr Paul Edeh: open plan living on the ground floor, three bedroom suites on the first, and a guest suite, family lounge and studio in the attic under three dormers. I designed the shell and the main floors. This stage took the model from bare walls to an issued P01 set in UK ISO 19650 style, with 43 rooms, a rebuilt loft stair, three families of my own and 16 sheets.

Rooms and areas

Room data and schedule of accommodation

0→43
rooms, numbered by floor, 718 m² in total

01What was wrong

The model held walls and little else: no rooms, one sample sheet, a level named Rool, default view names and 18 warnings. A house without rooms cannot be tagged, scheduled or checked.

02What I decided

Number rooms by floor, 01.xx to 03.xx, from my written layout, and give each a Use value so the schedule groups living, sleeping, sanitary, service and circulation. The attic rooms sit under a slope, so the room computation height went to 1.2 m; at the default the sloped rooms do not enclose.

03What changed

43 rooms with tags on every plan. A double run of the room script made 28 zero area duplicates. I deleted them and wrote every later script to be safe to rerun.

Drawing production

16 sheet P01 issue to BS EN ISO 19650

1→16
sheets, each with a container name, a status and a revision that agree

01What was wrong

One sample sheet with a sample revision on the cover. A set that is meant to read like a real issue needs every sheet to carry a number, a status and a revision that match the register.

02What I decided

Name every sheet to BS EN ISO 19650 with the UK National Annex fields, and number by drawing type so the register sorts itself. One Mejarc A1 title block carries S2, SHARED and Portfolio issue at revision P01. View templates fix plans at 1:50, elevations at 1:100 and sections at 1:50, with crops and datum extents set per view.

03What changed

16 sheets at P01. Five came in a second pass: the foundation plan, three reflected ceiling plans and a 3D perspectives sheet. Their viewports first landed off the sheet because the ceiling plans picked up distant site lines; cropping each to its floor plan's crop fixed it.

04What I would do next

Fix the dates. The title block date field prints 11/23/26 while the revision table on the same sheet says 23/11/2026, and the cover still shows 01/03/26. A UK reader sees a 23rd month. The next issue uses one format, ISO 8601, on every sheet.

Attic design coordination

Loft stair, gable wall and ceiling coordination

3.45 m
minimum headroom over the rebuilt loft stair, against 2.0 m in Approved Document K

01What was wrong

The loft stair arrived against the dormer cheek with no top landing. The gables had no inner leaf above 7050: the external wall type is vertically compound and its blockwork stops just under the attic floor, so the attic walls cleaned back to a face that did not exist and left a 110 mm strip. Nobody had drawn a ceiling or a way into the roof void.

02What I decided

Check before moving anything. Rays cast up from every tread showed the headroom was fine and the landing was the problem, so the flight was rebuilt instead of rerouted: 20 risers at 180 mm, a 250 mm going and winders round a small newel. The gables got a 110 mm inner leaf type from 7050 to the roof. Editing the wall type's layers would be cleaner, but it changes every wall of that type, so I held it back.

03What changed

A 965 mm top landing with a 900 mm guard on the well, gables with a real inner leaf, a flat collar ceiling at 2.7 m with the dormers vaulted, and a 600 by 700 loft hatch into the void. Sections CC and DD document each one.

04What I would do next

Close what is still open. The FD20 door to the stair enclosure was lost in a later edit, the foyer and landing need a fire screen or sprinklers to complete the protected route, and the W trusses must become attic trusses or a cut roof.

Families and presentation

Parametric families for wardrobes, WCs and the loft hatch

3
parametric families built for this house: fitted wardrobe, close coupled WC, loft hatch

01What was wrong

No residential WC, shower or fitted wardrobe family existed on the machine, so the first issue went out with empty WCs and loose shelving drawn in the walk in closets.

02What I decided

Build them small and parametric. The wardrobe is Casework, with width, depth and height driven by aligned reference planes. The WC is a 400 by 660 close coupled pan. The hatch is a lining and panel set in an opening cut in the collar ceiling. Every instance carries a Comments code, MEJ_FF_03 or MEJ_FF_04, so it can be filtered and scheduled.

03What changed

Six wardrobes replaced the shelving in four walk in closets. Side units in closets 2 and 3 were dropped because a 600 deep unit on that wall blocks the door. Five WCs went in, with Bathrooms 2 and 3 back to back on one soil stack.

04What I would do next

Add a shower tray family so the guest shower room stops borrowing a bath, and set subcategories on all three families so they print at the right line weight.

Why build, not download

A downloaded family brings its own materials, subcategories and hidden nested parts. Three small families I can read line by line cost less to check than one I cannot.

Caught on the way

Instances placed with a level and an absolute height landed at double the elevation. Later placements pass a zero offset from the level.
